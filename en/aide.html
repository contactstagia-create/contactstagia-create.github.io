The apps

GigFlow Live and InBeat (Android apps): yes, completely offline. No connection is required during use — in rehearsal, on the move, or on stage. InBeat also runs in the browser: the page loads online the first time, then can work offline (see the “InBeat in your browser” question below). GigFlow Editor is a web tool: it opens in your browser (a connection is needed to open it) and your data stays stored on your device.
The minimum: a mobile device, phone or tablet. GigFlow Live and InBeat are Android apps, from Android 7.0 onward, available on the Google Play Store. InBeat also runs in the browser (for example on iPhone or on a computer), as a trial version. GigFlow Editor opens in your browser, on a computer (PC or Mac), to prepare your songs and setlists — no installation needed.
No. All your data stays on your device. There's no account, no cloud, no automatic sync of your data (only InBeat's device-to-device tempo sync, in beta, runs over your local Wi-Fi network). You keep full control of your songs and setlists. To avoid any loss when switching devices or resetting your phone, remember to export your data regularly from the app.
It lets you try InBeat and rehearse without installing anything, notably on iPhone, iPad or a computer. On stage, the Android app remains the complete version. Limits: Master/Slave device sync is Android-only; keep the screen on and InBeat in the foreground (if you leave it, reopen it and restart the song); compatibility is not guaranteed on older iPhones and browsers. Your setlists stay on the device, inside the browser: export them regularly, because a browser may erase the data of a rarely visited site. On iPhone, “Share” then “Add to Home Screen” gives a full-screen display and offline use. The browser version measures its audience with your consent, unlike the Android apps (see the privacy policy). Open InBeat in your browser.

Usage

In GigFlow Editor, open the ☰ menu → “Export a backup” (or a song's or setlist's export button) to generate a file. Transfer it to your Android device (via cable, Bluetooth, e-mail…) then import it into GigFlow Live or InBeat from the app's import menu.
Yes. Export them from InBeat (.inb file), then in GigFlow Editor: ☰ menu → “Import a backup”. Songs arrive with their tempo, time signature, duration and notes, without lyrics or chords — to be completed in the editor. A song or setlist that is already there is not imported twice.
Yes: ☰ menu → “Trash”. Deleted songs and setlists stay there for 30 days and can be restored. And if the browser closes before you save, Editor offers to recover your changes the next time you open it.
Your songs and setlists are born in GigFlow Editor: lyrics and chords are composed with keyboard, mouse and a big screen. You then import them into GigFlow Live, the stage screen, to read them in the moment. In Live, you build and reorder your setlists from your library, and find the sheet of each song: title, artist, key, duration, tempo, time signature, category and capo position.
GigFlow Live and InBeat are compatible with Bluetooth pedals. In InBeat, the pedal moves to the next or the previous song. In GigFlow Live, it first moves to the next section, then to the next song; going the other way, it goes back to the previous section, then to the previous song. You keep your hands free while you play.
